What Playventure is about

Adventure is really about curiosity: going somewhere, finding something, solving a small mystery. That feeling does not need a faraway place. A garden, a park, a living room or the pages of a story can all become the setting for a good adventure.

Playventure was created to share simple, low-cost ideas for adventure play at home and outdoors, and to explain adventure-style games in plain language. We are a reading resource only and do not organise trips, sell equipment or run activities.

Kinds of adventure play

Adventure play comes in many shapes. Here are some of the most popular, all of which work with little or no special equipment.

Hunt

Treasure and scavenger hunts

Hide small items or write a list of things to find. Clues, maps and riddles add the sense of a real quest.

Outdoors

Nature exploration

Walk a local trail or park and look for leaves, birds, insects or interesting stones. Keeping a notebook turns a stroll into an expedition.

Puzzle

Puzzle quests

Link riddles, codes and simple locks into a chain where each answer reveals the next step, similar to an escape-style challenge.

Imagination

Pretend and role play

Children become explorers, pirates or space travellers. A cardboard box and a blanket can be a ship or a cave.

Active

Obstacle courses

Use cushions, chairs or garden items to build a course for crawling, balancing and jumping, with time challenges for fun.

Story

Adventure stories and games

Books, tabletop stories and video games let players explore imaginary worlds, make choices and follow a plot.

Plan a family quest in five legs

A good adventure day has a simple shape. Follow this trail to organise one for children or friends.

Choose a theme

Pick a story such as a lost treasure, a jungle expedition or a secret agent mission. A theme ties all the activities together.

Pick a place and a time

Choose a safe, familiar location such as a garden, a park or a few rooms at home. Keep the quest to an hour or two for younger children.

Create clues and tasks

Write short clues matched to the ages of the players. Mix thinking tasks, movement tasks and teamwork tasks.

Gather simple supplies

Paper, pencils, a basket, a torch and some water are often enough. Prepare a small prize or a shared snack for the end.

Celebrate and reflect

Finish with a treat and talk about favourite moments. Encourage everyone to suggest the next adventure.

Adventure games on screen and table

Adventure games invite players to explore, collect clues and solve problems, usually following a story. Some are played on a screen, others at a table with cards, maps and a shared storyteller.

When choosing one, look at the age rating, the pace and how much reading or puzzle solving is involved. Playing together at first helps you judge whether a game suits your household, and sharing a puzzle can be a good way to connect.

Qualities to look for

  • A clear story and goal that holds attention
  • Puzzles that feel fair rather than frustrating
  • An age rating that suits the player
  • Options to play at a comfortable pace
  • Clear information on any optional purchases

Adventure with care

Good preparation keeps adventures fun. These habits matter most when children or outdoor spaces are involved.

Supervise young explorers

Children should be accompanied by a responsible adult, especially near roads, water or unfamiliar ground.

Tell someone your plan

Let a family member know where you are going and when you expect to return, even for a short outing.

Dress for the weather

Wear comfortable footwear, bring water and use sun or rain protection as needed.

Respect nature and property

Stay on paths, leave plants and animals undisturbed, take litter home and ask permission before using private land.

Check the surroundings

Look out for hazards such as uneven ground, sharp objects or deep water before starting an activity.

Balance screen time

If adventure games are part of the day, agree on limits and mix them with outdoor and creative play.

Frequently asked questions

Is Playventure a travel agency or activity provider?

No. Playventure does not organise trips, sell equipment or run activities. It is a reading resource that shares ideas about adventure play in simple language.

Do I need special equipment for adventure play?

Usually not. Paper, pencils, a basket, a torch and everyday household items are enough for most treasure hunts and quests.

What age is suitable for a treasure hunt?

Young children enjoy very simple hunts with picture clues, while older children and adults enjoy riddles and codes. Adjust the difficulty to the youngest player.

How can I run an adventure on a rainy day?

Move the quest indoors. Hide clues around the house, build a blanket fort base camp or create a puzzle trail between rooms.

How do I keep an adventure fair for mixed ages?

Play in mixed teams, give older players roles that help younger ones and include tasks that use different strengths, such as reading, running or drawing.
